
# Supply Chain Security

**Project:** Western Interconnection Cyber Defense
**Course:** CS 03500 — Foundations of Cybersecurity
**Project Phase:** Supply Chain Security & Threat Intelligence
**Project Type:** Academic Cybersecurity Simulation

## Overview

This phase evaluated cybersecurity risks affecting the supply chain supporting the Western Interconnection power grid. The assessment focused on how vulnerabilities within utilities, vendors, software, and operational technology could be exploited to disrupt critical infrastructure.

The analysis examined three primary supply chain security concerns:

1. **SCADA and Industrial Control System Security**
2. **Third-Party Software and Vendor Risk**
3. **Limited Supply Chain Visibility and Transparency**

The assessment also identified authoritative cybersecurity resources that electricity providers can use to monitor emerging threats and security issues.

## Key Security Risks

### 1. SCADA and Industrial Control System Security

Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems are essential for monitoring and controlling electricity generation and distribution.

Compromise of administrative access to SCADA environments could allow an attacker to manipulate operational processes and potentially disrupt electricity delivery.

The analysis examined the 2015–2016 Russian cyberattacks against Ukraine's power grid as an example of how attackers can abuse legitimate access to operational systems.

**Security considerations:**

* Strong authentication and access controls
* Least-privilege access
* Protection of administrative accounts
* Monitoring of privileged activity
* Segmentation of operational technology environments

### 2. Third-Party Software and Vendor Risk

Utilities depend on third-party software, hardware, and services to operate and maintain critical infrastructure. Compromised software or vendor infrastructure can introduce security weaknesses into otherwise trusted environments.

The analysis examined the SolarWinds compromise as an example of a supply chain attack in which attackers compromised trusted software distribution mechanisms.

**Security considerations:**

* Vendor security assessments
* Third-party risk management
* Software and component verification
* Security testing before deployment
* Continuous monitoring of suppliers
* Defined cybersecurity requirements for vendors

### 3. Supply Chain Visibility and Transparency

Utilities may have limited visibility into the cybersecurity practices of suppliers and smaller third-party providers.

This lack of visibility can make it difficult to identify vulnerabilities before they are exploited.

**Security considerations:**

* Supplier risk assessments
* Improved vendor communication
* Supply chain monitoring
* Lifecycle risk management
* Security requirements for third parties
* Continuous evaluation of supplier security posture

## Threat Intelligence Resources

The assessment identified five organizations that can help energy-sector organizations monitor cybersecurity threats and security guidance:

| Organization | Purpose                                                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CISA         | Cybersecurity alerts, advisories, and critical infrastructure guidance |
| DOE CESER    | Energy-sector cybersecurity and emergency response resources           |
| NIST         | Cybersecurity frameworks, standards, and guidance                      |
| NERC         | Reliability and cybersecurity standards for the bulk power system      |
| NIST NCCoE   | Practical cybersecurity guidance and implementation resources          |

## Security Recommendations

Based on the assessment, critical infrastructure organizations should:

* Strengthen access controls for SCADA and operational technology environments
* Conduct security assessments of third-party vendors
* Validate and test third-party software and components
* Improve supply chain visibility
* Establish cybersecurity requirements for suppliers
* Continuously monitor emerging threats
* Apply risk management throughout the supplier lifecycle
* Improve communication and collaboration with vendors
